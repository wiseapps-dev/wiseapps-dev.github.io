How to space fence pickets evenly

Updated 2026-11-20 · Written by a tradie · Calculator: Picket fence spacing calculator

To space fence pickets evenly edge to edge, add the gap to the run length and divide by the picket width plus the gap. Round up for a maximum gap, or for a target gap keep the count whose gap is closest. The exact gap is the length minus all the picket widths, divided by one less than the count.

A picket fence looks right when every gap matches and the end pickets sit flush with the rail ends. This guide covers picket and fence paling spacing in millimetres and inches, target gap versus maximum gap, and shrinkage.

Pick your layout first

  • Edge to edge: a picket at both ends, flush with the ends of the rail or panel. This is the usual layout for pickets or palings fixed across the face of the rails. There's one fewer gap than pickets.
  • Between posts: the pickets sit between two posts with a gap at each post, like balusters. For that, use the formula in how to space balusters evenly.

This guide uses edge to edge.

The picket spacing formula (edge to edge)

L = the length of the rail or panel, w = picket width, g = gap, n = number of pickets.

  1. Count: n = (L + g) ÷ (w + g). With a maximum gap, round up. With a target gap, try the whole number below and the one above, and keep whichever gives the gap closest to what you want.
  2. Exact gap: g = (L − n × w) ÷ (n − 1).
  3. Marks: the left edge of picket i sits at (i − 1) × (w + g) from the start.

The free picket fence spacing calculator does these sums as you type and draws the panel, in millimetres or inches.

Target gap or maximum gap?

  • Use a maximum gap when the gap must never be bigger, for example for privacy or to keep a small dog in. You'll often land a little under the number.
  • Use a target gap when it's about looks. If you say "about 20 mm", you'd be happy with 19.6 or 21. The closest count might land slightly over or under.

Step by step

  1. Measure the run the pickets will cover, end to end. That's usually the rail length, unless the pickets overhang or stop short.
  2. Measure a few pickets. Widths vary by a millimetre or two, so use the typical width.
  3. Work out the count, then the exact gap.
  4. Work out every mark from the start of the run, and mark them all from one tape hook.
  5. Fix the two end pickets first, then fill in between.

Worked example 1: 2400 mm panel, 70 mm pickets, about 20 mm gaps

  • n = (2400 + 20) ÷ (70 + 20) = 2420 ÷ 90 = 26.89, so try 26 and 27.
  • 26 pickets: (2400 − 26 × 70) ÷ 25 = (2400 − 1820) ÷ 25 = 580 ÷ 25 = 23.2 mm.
  • 27 pickets: (2400 − 27 × 70) ÷ 26 = (2400 − 1890) ÷ 26 = 510 ÷ 26 = 19.6 mm.
  • 19.6 is closer to 20, so use 27 pickets at 19.6 mm. Centres are 70 + 19.6 = 89.6 mm.
  • Check: 27 × 70 + 26 × (510 ÷ 26) = 1890 + 510 = 2400 mm. ✓

Here are the marks to the left edge of each picket, rounded to the millimetre:

0, 90, 179, 269, 358, 448, 538, 627, 717, 807, 896, 986, 1075, 1165, 1255, 1344, 1434, 1523, 1613, 1703, 1792, 1882, 1972, 2061, 2151, 2240, 2330.

The last picket runs from 2330 to 2400. ✓

Worked example 2: the same panel, target gap vs maximum gap

Now say you want about 22 mm.

  • n = (2400 + 22) ÷ (70 + 22) = 2422 ÷ 92 = 26.33.
  • As a target: 26 pickets give 23.2 mm (1.2 over) and 27 give 19.6 mm (2.4 under). The closest is 26 at 23.2 mm.
  • As a maximum: round 26.33 up to 27, which gives 19.6 mm. You can't use 26, because 23.2 is over 22.

Same number, same panel, different answer. Decide which you mean before you order: one picket per panel adds up over a long fence.

Worked example 3: 96 inch section, 3 1/2 inch pickets, about 2 inch gaps

  • n = (96 + 2) ÷ (3.5 + 2) = 98 ÷ 5.5 = 17.82, so try 17 and 18.
  • 17 pickets: (96 − 17 × 3.5) ÷ 16 = (96 − 59.5) ÷ 16 = 36.5 ÷ 16 = 2.28 in.
  • 18 pickets: (96 − 18 × 3.5) ÷ 17 = (96 − 63) ÷ 17 = 33 ÷ 17 = 1.94 in.
  • 1.94 is closer to 2, so use 18 pickets at 1.94 in (a hair over 1 15/16"). Centres are 3.5 + 1.94 = 5.44 in (about 5 7/16").
  • Check: 18 × 3.5 + 17 × (33 ÷ 17) = 63 + 33 = 96 in. ✓

Here are the marks from the start, to the nearest 1/16":

0, 5 7/16, 10 7/8, 16 5/16, 21 3/4, 27 3/16, 32 5/8, 38 1/16, 43 1/2, 49, 54 7/16, 59 7/8, 65 5/16, 70 3/4, 76 3/16, 81 5/8, 87 1/16, 92 1/2.

The last picket ends at 92 1/2 + 3 1/2 = 96". ✓

Stepping 5 7/16" along seventeen times lands at 92 7/16", 1/16" short. On a longer run, or with rougher rounding, that grows. Working each mark from the start stops errors stacking up.

Allow for shrinkage

Timber shrinks across its width as it dries. Timber Queensland's data sheet on moisture puts average shrinkage across the growth rings, from green to 12% moisture content, at anywhere from under 3.5% to over 8%, depending on species. It also notes that backsawn boards shrink more in width than quartersawn boards.

Whatever width each picket loses becomes extra gap. Take 5% on a 70 mm green picket: 70 × 0.05 = 3.5 mm. The 19.6 mm gaps from example 1 open up to about 23 mm.

If your pickets are green and you want roughly 20 mm once they've dried, set them up tighter. Aim for about 16.5 mm:

  • n = (2400 + 16.5) ÷ (70 + 16.5) = 2416.5 ÷ 86.5 = 27.94, so try 27 and 28.
  • 27 gives 19.6 mm and 28 gives (2400 − 28 × 70) ÷ 27 = 440 ÷ 27 = 16.3 mm. Use 28.
  • After 3.5 mm of shrinkage, that's about 16.3 + 3.5 = 19.8 mm.

The same goes for butted palings. If you fix green palings tight together, gaps will open as they dry: a 150 mm paling losing 5% gives up 7.5 mm. Kiln-dried timber moves much less, but it still swells and shrinks with the weather.

FAQ

What gap should I leave between fence pickets? There's no one right answer for an ordinary fence. Go smaller for privacy and bigger for airflow and a lighter look. Choose roughly what you want, then let the sum set the exact figure so every gap matches.

How many pickets do I need for a 2.4 m panel? With 70 mm pickets and gaps of about 20 mm, you need 27 (example 1). For other sizes, run the formula.

Can I just use a spacer block? Yes, for speed, but check it against your marks every five or so pickets. You can't easily cut a block to 19.6 mm. With a 20 mm block, example 1 runs 27 × 70 + 26 × 20 = 2410 mm, so the last picket hangs 10 mm past the end of the rail.

Do fence picket gaps have to meet any rules? Pool fences and balustrades do. In Australia, pool barriers are covered by AS 1926.1 and state pool laws, and balustrades by the building code. See balustrade gap rules. Don't assume a picket layout meets those rules without checking.

How many pickets can I cut from each length? See how to plan a cut list, which allows for saw kerf.
