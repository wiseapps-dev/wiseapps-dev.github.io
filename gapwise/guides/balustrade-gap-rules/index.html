Balustrade gap rules: 125 mm, 4 inch and 100 mm explained

Updated 2026-11-20 · Written by a tradie · Calculator: Balustrade spacing calculator (Australia)

Every balustrade gap rule is a sphere test: if a ball of a set size can pass through an opening, the opening fails. The limit is 125 mm in Australia, 4 inches under the US residential code, and 100 mm in New Zealand housing and in English buildings that under-fives may use. Stay under the number, not on it.

This is a plain-English summary of balustrade spacing regulations, not legal advice. Rules differ by building type, change, and are adopted on different dates. Confirm with your certifier, inspector or building control body before you build.

Australia: NCC Housing Provisions, Part 11.3

For houses and other Class 1 and 10 buildings, the deemed-to-satisfy barrier rules are in Part 11.3 of the ABCB Housing Provisions.

  • When you need a barrier: where it's possible to fall 1 m or more, measured from the trafficable surface to the surface beneath (11.3.3(1)).
  • Height: at least 865 mm above stair nosings, and at least 1 m above landings, balconies, decks, verandahs and the like (11.3.4(2)).
  • Openings: openings in barriers, including decorative balustrades, must not let a 125 mm sphere pass. On stairs, the opening is measured above the nosing line (11.3.4(4)).
  • Attic-type stairs: stairs to a non-habitable room that isn't used regularly can stop a 300 mm sphere instead, or use rails no more than 460 mm apart (11.3.4(7)).
  • Face-fixed balustrades: the gap between the deck or stair edge and the barrier must not exceed 40 mm, measured horizontally (11.3.4(5) and (6)).
  • Climbing: where the fall is more than 4 m, horizontal elements between 150 mm and 760 mm above the floor must not make climbing easy (11.3.4(8)).

The balustrade spacing calculator works out the baluster count and gap for a run, kept under 125 mm.

Apartments and commercial buildings come under NCC Volume One, clauses D3D17 to D3D20. They also use the 125 mm sphere, with a 300 mm allowance for fire-isolated exits and some Class 7 and 8 buildings.

Which edition? These clause numbers are from NCC 2022. Victoria adopted NCC 2025 on 1 May 2026, and other states are adopting it on their own dates. The ABCB's preview draft of the 2025 Housing Provisions lists only two changes to Part 11.3, both about AS 1657 for some Class 10b structures. Check which edition applies to your permit.

New Zealand: F4 and F4/AS1 (100 mm, not 125 mm)

New Zealand doesn't match Australia here. Clause F4 needs a barrier where people could fall 1 m or more (F4.3.1). Where children under 6 are likely, the barrier must restrict their passage (F4.3.4(g)).

The Acceptable Solution F4/AS1 (3rd edition, amendment 2, in force since 1 January 2017) covers housing and other areas likely to be frequented by children under 6. It says:

  • openings anywhere over the barrier's full height must stop a 100 mm sphere (1.2.1(b))
  • on stairs, the triangle formed by the riser, the tread and the bottom rail must stop a 150 mm sphere (1.2.1(c)).

Heights for houses (Table 1) are 900 mm on stairs, ramps and intermediate landings, and 1000 mm on balconies, decks and the edges of internal floors. Where children don't go, F4/AS1 allows 300 mm between vertical balusters or 460 mm between rails (1.2.2). Anything else is an alternative solution for your building consent authority to accept.

United States: IRC section R312

The International Residential Code covers one- and two-family homes and townhouses where a state or city adopts it. That's often with local amendments.

  • When you need a guard: on open-sided walking surfaces more than 30 inches above the floor or grade below, measured within 36 inches horizontally of the edge (R312.1.1).
  • Height: at least 36 inches, or 34 inches on the open sides of stairs (R312.1.2).
  • Openings: a 4 inch sphere must not pass (R312.1.3). There are two exceptions. Guards on the sides of stair treads must stop a 4 3/8 inch sphere. The triangle formed by the riser, the tread and the bottom rail must stop a 6 inch sphere.

Apartments and commercial buildings use the International Building Code instead.

United Kingdom: Approved Document K (England)

  • When you need guarding: where there's a drop of more than 600 mm in a dwelling, or more than 380 mm (or two risers) in other buildings (Requirement K2).
  • Height in single-family dwellings (Diagram 3.1): 900 mm for stairs, landings and internal floor edges, and 1100 mm for external balconies and roof edges.
  • Openings: in a building that children under five may use, a 100 mm sphere must not pass through the guarding. Guarding shouldn't be easy to climb, so avoid horizontal rails (paragraphs 1.39 and 3.3). Open risers in dwellings must also stop a 100 mm sphere (paragraph 1.9).

A toddler could live in or visit any home, so plan on 100 mm for houses unless your building control body says otherwise. Wales publishes its own Part K guidance, and Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own standards. For stair and landing spindles, the UK spindle spacing calculator keeps every gap under 100 mm.

Worked example 1: one run under 125 mm and under 100 mm

Posts 2400 mm apart, 42 mm balusters. The method is in how to space balusters evenly.

Under 125 mm (Australia):

  • Count: (2400 − 125) ÷ (42 + 125) = 2275 ÷ 167 = 13.62, which rounds up to 14.
  • Gap: (2400 − 14 × 42) ÷ 15 = 1812 ÷ 15 = 120.8 mm. ✓

Under 100 mm (NZ housing, UK):

  • Count: (2400 − 100) ÷ (42 + 100) = 2300 ÷ 142 = 16.20, which rounds up to 17.
  • Gap: (2400 − 17 × 42) ÷ 18 = 1686 ÷ 18 = 93.7 mm. ✓
  • One fewer (16) gives (2400 − 672) ÷ 17 = 101.6 mm, too wide.

That's three more balusters per panel. For an inch example (72 in between posts, 13 balusters at 3 3/4 in), see how to space balusters evenly.

Worked example 2: when the gap lands exactly on the limit

The posts are 2463 mm apart, the balusters are 42 mm wide, and the limit is 125 mm.

  • Count: (2463 − 125) ÷ 167 = 2338 ÷ 167 = 14.00 exactly.
  • With 14 balusters the gap is (2463 − 588) ÷ 15 = 1875 ÷ 15 = 125.0 mm. That's on the limit, not under it.
  • Add one: (2463 − 15 × 42) ÷ 16 = 1833 ÷ 16 = 114.6 mm. ✓

When the count comes out as a whole number, add one.

FAQ

What is the maximum balustrade gap in Australia? A 125 mm sphere must not pass through any opening. That includes decorative balustrades, and on stairs the opening is measured above the nosing line.

Is the balustrade gap in NZ the same as in Australia? No. Under F4/AS1, barriers in housing must stop a 100 mm sphere, and the stair triangle a 150 mm sphere.

What is the baluster spacing code in the US? Under IRC R312.1.3, the limit is a 4 inch sphere, or 4 3/8 inch on the sides of stair treads, and the stair triangle must stop a 6 inch sphere. Check your local amendments.

What are the spindle spacing regulations in the UK? In England, guarding in buildings that under-fives may use must stop a 100 mm sphere and shouldn't be climbable. Plan on 100 mm for homes. The UK spindle spacing calculator keeps every gap under it.
