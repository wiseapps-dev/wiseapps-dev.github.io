The US rule in one line

The International Residential Code (section R312, quoted here from the 2018 edition) says a required guard must not have openings, from the walking surface up to the required guard height, that let a 4 inch sphere through. So every gap between deck balusters has to be under 4 inches, not on it. The calculator's preset keeps every gap under 4 inches.

The rest of the guard rules for decks and porches

  • When you need a guard: on open-sided walking surfaces, including stairs, ramps and landings, more than 30 inches above the floor or grade below, measured at any point within 36 inches horizontally of the open side (R312.1.1).
  • Height: at least 36 inches above the walking surface on decks, porches, balconies and landings, and at least 34 inches above the line of the nosings on the open sides of stairs (R312.1.2).
  • Stairs: guards on the open side of stairs may have openings up to a 4 3/8 inch sphere, and the triangle formed by the riser, the tread and the bottom rail must stop a 6 inch sphere (R312.1.3, exceptions). For balusters on the stair, use the balusters per tread calculator.

This is a summary to help you plan, not building advice, and the calculator doesn't check that your work meets the rules. Rules vary by place and building type, change over time and are adopted on different dates. Check your local building code and your inspector, certifier or building control body before you build. Many states and cities adopt the IRC with their own amendments, and later editions renumber some sections.

Worked example: a 6 foot section

The posts are 72 inches apart and the balusters are 2x2s, which measure 1 1/2 inches. With balusters between two posts there is a gap at both ends, so n balusters make n + 1 gaps.

count   = (72 − 4) ÷ (1 1/2 + 4) = 12.4, round up to 13
gap     = (72 − 13 × 1 1/2) ÷ 14  = 3 3/4"
centres = 1 1/2 + 3 3/4           = 5 1/4"

Check one fewer: 12 balusters leave gaps of 4 5/32 inches, which is over the limit. An 8 foot section (96 inches between posts) needs 17 at 3 29/32 inches. Mark each baluster from the post face, not by stepping a spacer block along, so small errors don't add up.

Gap, not centres

The 4 inch rule is about the clear gap. People often say "4 inches on center", but with 1 1/2 inch balusters that leaves a 2 1/2 inch gap and uses far more balusters than you need. The centres here (5 1/4 inches) are just handy for marking out.

Questions

How many balusters do I need for an 8 foot deck railing?

With 1 1/2 inch balusters and 96 inches between the posts, 17, at a gap of 3 29/32 inches. Measure between your posts, not the post centres, and type the numbers into the calculator.

Does the 4 inch rule apply to porch railings?

Yes. The IRC guard rules cover porches, balconies, decks and landings more than 30 inches above the floor or grade below. Lower porches don't need a guard under the IRC, but many people follow the same spacing anyway.

Are aluminum and composite railing kits different?

Kits come with their own baluster spacing and brackets. Follow the maker's instructions, and check the finished gaps against the 4 inch limit.

Does the calculator make my railing pass inspection?

No. It keeps every gap under 4 inches, but heights, loads and local amendments matter too. Check with your building department.
