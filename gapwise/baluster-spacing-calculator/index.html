Baluster spacing calculator

To space balusters evenly between two posts, take the length between the posts minus your largest gap, divide by the baluster width plus that gap, and round up to get the count. The gap is then the length minus all the baluster widths, divided by the count plus one.

Worked example: 2400 mm between posts

The posts are 2400 mm apart, the balusters are 42 mm wide, and every gap must be under 125 mm. With balusters between two posts there is a gap at both ends, so n balusters make n + 1 gaps.

count   = (2400 − 125) ÷ (42 + 125) = 13.6, round up to 14
gap     = (2400 − 14 × 42) ÷ 15     = 120.8 mm
centres = 42 + 120.8                = 162.8 mm

Check one fewer: 13 balusters give (2400 − 546) ÷ 14 = 132.4 mm, which is over the limit. So 14 is the minimum.

Mark from the post, not gap by gap

If you round the gap to 121 and step it along the rail, the error adds up. By the last baluster you're 3 mm out. Instead, work out each mark from the post face: baluster i starts at i × gap + (i − 1) × width. That gives 120.8, 283.6, 446.4, 609.2 and so on. The calculator and the app both do it this way.

If the gap lands exactly on the limit, add one more baluster. The rules say a sphere must not pass, so exactly 125 mm isn't good enough.

Questions

How many balusters do I need for 2.4 m?

With 42 mm balusters under the 125 mm rule, you need 14. Under a 100 mm rule it's 17. For other sizes, type them into the calculator.

Is baluster spacing measured as the gap or as centres?

The rules limit the clear gap. Centres (gap plus baluster width) are just handy for marking out.

Does it work on stairs?

Yes. Use the horizontal distance between the posts, because the gap between vertical balusters is measured horizontally. Stairs have extra rules, covered in the balustrade gap rules guide. To set out the stair itself, use the stair calculator.
