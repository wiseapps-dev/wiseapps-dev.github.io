Equal spacing calculator

To space items evenly, first decide how the ends work: a gap at both ends, an item flush at both ends, or half a space at each end, as for downlights. Then enter the length, the item width and how many you want. The calculator gives the gap, the centres and the marks from the start.

Three ways to set out the ends

  • Gap at both ends: items between two fixed edges, like balusters between posts, shelves between a floor and a ceiling, or frames on a wall. n items make n + 1 gaps: gap = (L − n × w) ÷ (n + 1).
  • Item at both ends: the first and last items flush with the ends, like fence pickets, battens or slats. n items make n − 1 gaps: gap = (L − n × w) ÷ (n − 1).
  • Half a space at each end: the way downlights, joists from a centre line or holes on a rail are often set out. The centres are L ÷ n, and the first sits half a space in.

Worked examples

  • Six hooks on a 1200 mm board, 20 mm wide, a gap at both ends: centres (1200 + 20) ÷ 7 = 174.3 mm, gap 154.3 mm. Marks to the left edge of each hook: 154.3, 328.6, 502.9, 677.1.
  • Four downlights in a 3600 mm room, half a space at each end: centres 900 mm, the first 450 mm from the wall, then 1350, 2250 and 3150.
  • Three 500 mm frames on a 2400 mm wall, a gap at both ends: (2400 − 1500) ÷ 4 = 225 mm between the frames and at each end.

Mark from one end

Work out each mark from the start (item i starts at a fixed multiple of the centres), and measure every mark from the same end with one tape hook. Stepping a rounded gap along adds the rounding up: 12 steps of a gap rounded by half a millimetre end up 6 mm out. The calculator works every mark from the start.

Questions

How do I space things evenly on a wall?

Measure the space, decide whether you want a gap at both ends, then take the total width of the items away from the length and divide by the number of gaps (one more than the items). Mark every item from the same end.

What does centres mean?

Centre to centre: the distance from the middle of one item to the middle of the next. It's the gap plus one item width, and it is the easiest number to mark out with.

How do I space downlights evenly?

Divide the room length by the number of lights for the centres, and put the first one half that distance from the wall. Do the same across the room for a grid.
