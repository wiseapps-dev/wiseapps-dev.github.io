The NZ rule in one line

Acceptable Solution F4/AS1 (3rd edition, amendment 2, valid from 1 January 2017) says that in housing, and in other areas likely to be frequented by children under 6, openings anywhere over the full height of a barrier must be small enough that a 100 mm sphere can't pass through. So every gap between balusters has to be under 100 mm. That's tighter than Australia's 125 mm.

The rest of F4/AS1 for houses

  • Stairs: the triangle formed by the riser, the tread and the bottom rail of the barrier must stop a 150 mm sphere (1.2.1).
  • Heights in detached dwellings and within household units (Table 1): 900 mm on stairs, ramps and intermediate landings, and 1000 mm on balconies, decks and the edges of internal floors.
  • Climbing: the comments to clause 1.2.1 point out that horizontal or near-horizontal rails are easy for young children to climb, so vertical balusters are the usual choice.
  • Where children aren't expected (areas used only for emergencies or maintenance, and buildings not frequented by children), openings can be up to 300 mm between vertical balusters or 460 mm between rails (1.2.2).

This is a summary to help you plan, not building advice, and the calculator doesn't check that your work meets the rules. Rules vary by place and building type, change over time and are adopted on different dates. Check your local building code and your inspector, certifier or building control body before you build. F4/AS1 is one way to comply with clause F4 of the Building Code; your building consent authority decides.

Worked example: a 2.4 m deck edge

The posts are 2400 mm apart and the balusters are 42 mm wide. With balusters between two posts there is a gap at both ends, so n balusters make n + 1 gaps.

count   = (2400 − 100) ÷ (42 + 100) = 16.2, round up to 17
gap     = (2400 − 17 × 42) ÷ 18     = 93.7 mm
centres = 42 + 93.7                 = 135.7 mm

Check one fewer: 16 balusters give (2400 − 672) ÷ 17 = 101.6 mm, over the limit. Under Australia's 125 mm rule the same run would need only 14, which is why plans copied from Australian sources often fail in New Zealand. For balusters on the stair, use the balusters per tread calculator.

Questions

What is the maximum gap between balusters in New Zealand?

Under F4/AS1, a 100 mm sphere must not pass through any opening in a barrier in housing or where children under 6 are likely. Every gap must be under 100 mm.

How many balusters do I need for 2.4 m?

With 42 mm balusters and 2400 mm between the posts, 17 at a 93.7 mm gap. Type your own numbers into the calculator for the exact count.

Is a glass balustrade different?

Glass and its fixings have their own design rules. This calculator only spaces vertical balusters.
