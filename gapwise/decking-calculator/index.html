Decking board calculator

To lay deck boards, add one gap to the deck width and divide by the board width plus the gap, then round down for the full boards. The width left after those boards and their gaps is the rip for the last board. Or adjust the gap slightly so every board stays full width.

Worked example: a 3.6 m wide deck

The deck is 3600 mm across the boards. The boards are 140 mm wide with 5 mm gaps, and the first board sits flush with the edge.

full boards (3600 + 5) ÷ (140 + 5)
            = 24.9, round down to 24
used        24 × (140 + 5) = 3480 mm
last board  3600 − 3480 = 120 mm rip

That's 25 boards across, the last one ripped to 120 mm. If you'd rather not rip, 25 full boards fit with the gap closed up to (3600 − 25 × 140) ÷ 24 = 4.2 mm.

How many lengths to buy

Each board across is one row. With 5.4 m boards and a deck 2.4 m long, two rows come out of each board: 2 × 2400 plus 3 mm for the saw is 4803 mm. So 25 rows need 13 boards.

Make the deck 2.7 m long and two rows no longer fit in a 5.4 m board once the saw takes its 3 mm, so you need all 25. Rows longer than a board use whole boards plus a cut piece, and the offcuts go into other rows. Stagger the joins and land each one on a joist.

Avoid a thin last board

A rip under half a board looks odd and can split. Either nudge the gap so every board stays full width, or rip the first and last boards to the same width: add the board width to the rip and halve it. A 20 mm rip on 140 mm boards becomes two 80 mm boards.

Don't close the gap below what the board maker recommends. Boards need room for drainage and movement, so check the installation guide first.

Questions

How many deck boards do I need across my deck?

Add one gap to the deck width, divide by the board width plus the gap and round down for the full boards. If anything is left after the last gap, you need one more board, ripped to fit.

What gap should I leave between deck boards?

Use the gap in the board maker's installation guide. Gaps of around 3 mm to 6 mm are common, but hardwood, treated pine and composite boards each have their own recommendation.

Where should the ripped board go?

Most builders start with a full board on the edge people see most, often along the house or the front, and put the rip where it shows least. Or rip both edges evenly.

Does it work in inches?

Yes. Tap inches and type feet, inches and fractions, like 12' for the deck and 5 1/2 for the boards. Lengths to buy then allow 1/8 inch per cut.
