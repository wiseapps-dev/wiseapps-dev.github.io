Picket spacing calculator

To space pickets evenly with a picket flush at each end, add your largest gap to the panel length, divide by the picket width plus that gap, and round up to get the count. The gap is the length minus all the picket widths, divided by one less than the count.

Worked example: a 2400 mm panel

The panel is 2400 mm long, the pickets are 70 mm wide, and gaps can be no bigger than 25 mm. With a picket flush at both ends, n pickets make n − 1 gaps.

count   = (2400 + 25) ÷ (70 + 25) = 25.5, round up to 26
gap     = (2400 − 26 × 70) ÷ 25   = 23.2 mm
centres = 70 + 23.2               = 93.2 mm

Picket i starts at (i − 1) × centres from the start of the run: 0, 93.2, 186.4, 279.6 and so on. Mark them all from one tape hook so errors don't stack up.

Allow for shrinkage

Timber pickets shrink as they dry, so many builders start a little tighter than the gap they want. The same edge-to-edge layout works for battens, slats, cladding boards and decking. For a deck, the decking calculator also works out the last rip and the lengths to buy.

Questions

What gap should I leave between fence pickets?

There's no one right answer for an ordinary fence. Go smaller for privacy and bigger for airflow and a lighter look. Choose roughly what you want, then let the sum set the exact figure so every gap matches.

What if my pickets sit between posts?

Then there's a gap at each post, like balusters. Use the baluster spacing calculator instead.

Can I just use a spacer block?

Yes, for speed, but check it against your marks every five or so pickets. With a 20 mm block where the true gap is 19.6 mm, 27 pickets on a 2400 mm rail run 10 mm past the end.
