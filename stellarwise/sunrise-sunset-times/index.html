    What the columns mean

    • Sunrise and sunset: when the top edge of the Sun meets a level horizon, allowing for the way the air bends light near the horizon. Hills, buildings and your height change what you actually see by a few minutes.
    • First light and last light: civil twilight, when the Sun is 6 degrees below the horizon. Before first light and after last light you need lights to read outside.
    • Dark sky: from the end of astronomical twilight in the evening to its start next morning, when the Sun is more than 18 degrees down and adds no light to the sky. Faint stars and the Milky Way show most clearly then, if the Moon is down too.

    Close to midsummer, places far from the equator (London, Edinburgh, Stockholm, Dunedin) don’t get properly dark at all: the column says “No full darkness”. Inside the Arctic and Antarctic circles the Sun can stay up, or down, all day.

    The times come from the same astronomy code as the Stellarwise app and match timeanddate.com’s tables to the minute in our checks. For the Moon, see moonrise and moonset times; for what’s up after dark, see planets visible tonight.
