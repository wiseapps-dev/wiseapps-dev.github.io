Stargazing guides

Short, sourced guides for looking up: how to tell a planet from a star, why the Moon has phases and tilts, how to watch a meteor shower, how to watch a solar eclipse without hurting your eyes, and why your phone’s compass is sometimes off and how to fix it.

  • How to tell a planet from a starPlanets shine steadily, sit near the Sun's path and move against the stars. Five simple checks to tell a planet from a star, with or without an app.
  • Moon phases explainedWhy the Moon has phases, what waxing and waning mean, when each phase rises, and why the Moon looks upside down from the Southern Hemisphere.
  • How to watch a meteor showerWhere to look, when to go out and how long to let your eyes adjust, plus why the radiant's height and the Moon change how many meteors you see.
  • How to watch a solar eclipse safelyEclipse glasses that meet ISO 12312-2, pinhole projection, why sunglasses and unfiltered binoculars are unsafe, and the next total solar eclipses.
  • Why your phone’s compass is offPhone compasses drift because of nearby metal, magnets and magnetic declination. How to calibrate with a figure 8, and how star apps correct for it.

Eye safety. Never look at the Sun without proper eclipse glasses, and never point binoculars, a camera or a telescope at it without a proper solar filter on the front.
