    How to read the list

    • When: evening means it is up after dark and sets during the night; before dawn means it rises late and is best in the morning; all night means it is up from dusk to dawn.
    • Highest: the time in the night when the planet is highest, which is when you see it through the least air.
    • Where to look: the direction (north, south-east and so on) and the height above the horizon in degrees. Your fist held at arm’s length covers about 10 degrees.
    • Brightness: the magnitude. Smaller is brighter: Venus is around −4, Jupiter around −2, the brightest stars around 0, and the faintest stars you see from a town around 4.

    Uranus and Neptune are listed too, but you need binoculars for Uranus and a telescope for Neptune. Planets close to the Sun are lost in its glare, so a planet can be above the horizon and still not visible.
