Stellarwise support

Need help with Stellarwise? Most answers are below: the compass, location, the camera view, Pro and moving to a new phone, accuracy and alerts. If yours isn’t, email wiseapps.help+stellarwise@gmail.com with your phone model and what happened, and the developer will reply.

Is there a subscription?

No. Stellarwise is free to download. One optional in-app purchase, Stellarwise Pro, unlocks everything once for US$5.99. There is no subscription and there are no ads.

What is free, and what does Pro add?

Free: tonight’s sky for your place or any of 3,700 cities, from 6 hours ago to 24 hours ahead; the Sun, Moon and planets with rise and set times; 88 constellations; search; point-to-find with the compass; the Tonight page and the sky calendar; red night mode. Pro adds the camera view, any date and time, the 110 Messier objects, alerts, star-map posters and the observing log. A log you kept stays readable and exportable without Pro.

I bought Pro. How do I get it on a new phone?

Sign in with the same Apple ID or Google account, open Settings in Stellarwise and tap Restore purchases. Refunds are handled by Apple or Google.

The sky doesn’t line up with what I see

Phone compasses are usually right to within about 5 degrees and can be much worse near metal, magnets, cars or a magnetic case. Move the phone in a figure 8 a few times, step away from metal, or open the compass sheet and line it up on the Moon or a bright planet. The app shows how sure the compass is.

Why does it ask for my location?

Only if you tap Use my location. It is rounded to about a kilometre and stays on your phone; it is used to work out the sky above you. You can pick a city from the built-in list instead and never give location access.

Why does the camera view need the camera?

The camera view (Pro) shows the live picture from your back camera with names drawn over it. The picture is shown on the screen only; it is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere.

How accurate are the positions?

Positions come from Astronomy Engine and are tested against NASA JPL’s ephemerides: the planets and the Moon to well within a tenth of a degree, and rise and set times to within about a minute.

Do alerts need the internet?

No. Alerts (Pro) are planned on your phone for the next twelve months as local notifications. Open the app now and then to keep them topped up.

Can I look at the Sun with it?

Never look at the Sun without proper eclipse glasses, and never point binoculars, a camera or a telescope at it without a proper solar filter on the front. The app shows where the Sun is; it is not a solar filter.

Which languages does it support?

English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Portuguese (Portugal) and Swedish.

Still stuck?

Email wiseapps.help+stellarwise@gmail.com. Include your phone model and the app version (Settings, under About).
