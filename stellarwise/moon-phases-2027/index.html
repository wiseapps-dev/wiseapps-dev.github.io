    The four main phases

    A new Moon is the moment the Moon is between the Earth and the Sun, so its lit side faces away from us and it can’t be seen. About a week later, at first quarter, half of the disc is lit in the evening sky. The full Moon is opposite the Sun and rises around sunset; a week after that, at last quarter, the other half is lit in the morning sky. One cycle takes about 29.5 days, so most months have one of each and some have two.

    2027 at a glance

    5 of the 2027 new and full Moons bring an eclipse: Sat, Feb 6, annular solar; Sat, Feb 20, penumbral lunar; Sun, Jul 18, penumbral lunar; Mon, Aug 2, total solar; Tue, Aug 17, penumbral lunar. Dark, moonless nights for stargazing fall in the few days around each new Moon.
