    What the events mean

    • Opposition: an outer planet is opposite the Sun in our sky, so it rises at sunset, is up all night and is near its brightest.
    • Greatest elongation: Mercury or Venus is as far from the Sun as it gets, so it is easiest to see, low in the evening sky after sunset or the morning sky before sunrise.
    • Close together: two planets, or the Moon and a planet, pass close to each other in the sky. Separations are measured from the centre of the Earth, so they can look a little different from your place. Pairings close to the Sun are left out.
    • Meteor shower peak: the rate is for a perfectly dark sky with the radiant overhead; real counts are lower, especially with a bright Moon. The meteor shower calendar works out what you might see from your place.
