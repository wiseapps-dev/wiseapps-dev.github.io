    What the numbers mean

    The peak rate, or ZHR (zenithal hourly rate), is how many meteors one observer would see in an hour under a perfectly dark sky with the shower’s radiant straight overhead. Real counts are lower. With the radiant 30 degrees up you see about half; at 10 degrees, about a sixth. The column for your place uses that rule at the darkest good hour of the peak night.

    Peak dates follow the International Meteor Organization’s working list. Showers can peak a little early or late, and some, like the Draconids, vary a lot from year to year.
