Moon phase on any date

Enter a date, such as a birthday or a wedding day, to see the Moon’s phase that night: its name, how much of it was lit, its age and how it looked. It works for any date from 1900 to 2100, and shows the years the same phase falls on that date again.

    How it works

    The phase comes from where the Moon and Sun are in the sky at the date and time you choose, worked out with the same astronomy code as the Stellarwise app. The age is the number of days since the last new Moon, and a whole cycle from new Moon to new Moon takes about 29.5 days.

    The time matters a little: the lit part changes by up to about 5 percentage points in 12 hours. If you know the hour, enter it with the place, so the date and time are read in that place’s time zone. With no time, 9 pm is used.

    Why the same phase comes back

    A calendar year is about 11 days longer than 12 lunar cycles, so the phase on a date shifts by about 11 days a year. After 19 years, 235 lunar cycles take almost exactly 19 calendar years (the Metonic cycle), so the Moon on a birthday looks almost the same at ages 19, 38, 57 and 76.

    Which way round it is lit

    From the Northern Hemisphere a waxing Moon is lit on the right and a waning Moon on the left. From the Southern Hemisphere it is the other way round. The drawing follows the place you choose. Read moon phases explained for why.
