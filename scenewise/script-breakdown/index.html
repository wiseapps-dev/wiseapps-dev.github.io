Script breakdown: lines per character and scene chart

Paste a script to count each character's lines and spoken words, their share of the dialogue and a rough speaking time, with a chart of who speaks in each scene. It helps directors and teachers balance parts, spot roles that could be doubled and plan rehearsals. Free, with no sign-up.

Put each name in capitals before the line, and scene headings on their own line, like SCENE 2. The garden. Formatting tips

Rehearsing the part, not just counting it? The Scenewise app imports PDF, Word and Final Draft scripts, reads the other characters aloud and tracks which of your scenes you know.

What it counts

  • Lines are speeches: everything a character says before someone else speaks.
  • Words are spoken words only. Stage directions in brackets aren't counted.
  • Speaking time is the words at 140 a minute, the pace the Scenewise app assumes for your lines. Real scenes run longer, with pauses, business and reactions.
  • The scene chart shows who speaks in each scene and how many lines. Characters who are on stage but silent won't show, so check entrances in the script.

Using it to cast and schedule

Directors and teachers use a breakdown to balance parts across a cast, to see who could double two small roles because they never meet, and to plan rehearsals so nobody waits around for a scene they aren't in. Save it as a CSV file to open in a spreadsheet. It runs in your browser: nothing you paste is uploaded or stored.

Once the parts are handed out, actors can make their own cue script, or you can send the script to your cast as a link.
