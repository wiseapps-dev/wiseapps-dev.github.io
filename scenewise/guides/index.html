Guides to learning lines

These guides cover how actors learn lines: memorising fast, learning a monologue, getting off book for a play, preparing sides the night before an audition, running lines without a scene partner, self-taping without a reader and the first-letter method. They draw on actor practice and memory research, with sources, and one covers laying out a script for an app.

  1. How to memorise lines fast: what actually works

    How actors really learn lines, and what memory research adds: meaning first, small chunks, testing yourself, spacing and sleep.

  2. How to memorise a monologue

    Choose well, map the thoughts, learn it in sections, use first letters, and make it survive audition nerves.

  3. How to learn lines for a play: a rehearsal timeline

    From read-through to opening night: a week-by-week timeline, the off-book date, cue lines, calling for a line and line notes.

  4. How to run lines without a scene partner

    Six ways to run lines alone, from recording your cues to the cover card, plus the mistakes that waste solo practice.

  5. How to self-tape without a reader

    A friend in the room or on a video call, a paid reader, or recorded lines: when each fits, plus where the reader sits and how loud.

  6. The first-letter method for memorising text

    Reduce each word to its first letter, recall from the letters, then fade them out. How it works and why it helps.

  7. Learning lines the night before an audition or self-tape

    Sides the night before an audition or self-tape: how off book to be, what to learn first, an evening plan and why sleep matters.

  8. How to format a script so an app can read it

    Names in capitals, NAME: line, directions in brackets, scene headings, and why scanned PDFs can't be read.
