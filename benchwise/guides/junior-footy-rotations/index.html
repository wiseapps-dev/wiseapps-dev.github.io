Junior Footy Rotations: Fair Game Time in Aussie Rules

Junior footy rotations share the time through the interchange and across the three zones. The AFL's national junior rules suggest rotating at least every quarter, with a 50 to 75% game time minimum set by each league. With 12 on the ground, four 12-minute quarters and 16 players, everyone plays 36 minutes, or 75%.

Updated 2026-11-21. Written by the maker of Benchwise.

Junior footy has more moving parts than most kids' sports. You have twelve or more on the ground, three zones, an interchange, four quarters, and a crowd of parents who can all count. A rotation plan keeps it fair and keeps you sane.

This guide covers what the national junior rules say, how many minutes each player gets for squads of 13 to 18, and a simple routine for interchange and zones.

What the national junior rules say

The AFL publishes national junior match rules for community football, with a handbook that leagues adapt. The age group settings in the May 2024 handbook are:

Age group On the ground Most in the team Quarters Zones
Under 8 6 9 4 × 10 min 3 equal zones
Under 9 9 12 4 × 12 min 3 equal zones
Under 10 12 15 4 × 12 min 3 equal zones
Under 11 12 15 4 × 15 min not set
Under 12 12 15 4 × 15 min not set

Older age groups move up to 15 a side, and eventually 16 or 18. There are also modified rules for tackling, bouncing and marking at each age, and no scores, ladders or finals in the youngest groups.

Two parts of the handbook matter most for rotations:

  1. Rotate at least every quarter, so players get chances in several positions, including on the interchange.
  2. Minimum game time of 50 to 75%, with the exact percentage set by the controlling body (your league). Injured players are excepted.

Leagues adapt these rules for local conditions, and many publish their own junior by-laws. Your league's version is the one that applies. Check it for the exact minimum, team sizes and any interchange rules before the season.

Why the minimum matters

Kids stay in sport when they get to play. The Australian Sports Commission's Clearinghouse for Sport notes that sport which feels too serious or too competitive is a common reason children drop out, especially those who are still developing. A game-time minimum is the floor, not the target. If you can share the time equally, do.

Minutes for squads of 13 to 18

With 12 on the ground for four 12-minute quarters (the under 10 setting), there are 12 × 48 = 576 player-minutes to share. Interchange about every 6 minutes and each quarter has 2 stints, so the game has 8 stints and 96 places.

Squad Average Fair split (6-minute stints) Share of the game
13 44.3 min 5 play 48, 8 play 42 88 to 100%
14 41.1 min 12 play 42, 2 play 36 75 to 88%
15 38.4 min 6 play 42, 9 play 36 75 to 88%
16 36 min everyone plays 36 75%
17 33.9 min 11 play 36, 6 play 30 63 to 75%
18 32 min 6 play 36, 12 play 30 63 to 75%

Every split adds up to 96 stints, and every player is within or above the 50 to 75% range. Squads of 16 to 18 are bigger than the national maximum for under 10 to under 12, so those rows suit older age groups or local variations.

With 4 × 15-minute quarters (under 11 and under 12), change every 7 minutes 30 seconds to keep 2 stints per quarter, or every 5 minutes for 3.

A routine for interchange and zones

Before the game

  • Make the plan. Who starts on the interchange, who comes on at each change, and which zone each player starts in.
  • Plan zones by quarter. Rotating at least once a quarter is the national guidance, and the quarter break is the easiest time to do it. A simple pattern: each player moves from back to mid to forward (or the reverse) across the quarters, with interchange time slotted in.
  • Pick an interchange helper. A parent at the interchange with the plan, counting players on and off, is worth their weight in oranges.

During each quarter

  • Change at the planned time. Follow your league's interchange rules. Where rolling interchange during play is allowed, make sure the player coming off is off before the next one runs on.
  • Swap like for like. Mid-quarter, the player coming on usually takes the zone of the player coming off. Save big reshuffles for the breaks.
  • Count heads after every change. Twelve on the ground means twelve.

At the breaks

  • Move the zones. Quarter time, half time and three-quarter time are when you move kids between back, mid and forward.
  • Check the tally. A quick look at who has had the least time so far tells you who must start the next quarter on the ground.

Positions beyond the zones

Fair game time isn't only about minutes. Try to give every player:

  • a turn in the centre square or the midfield, where the ball is
  • a turn forward, where the goals are kicked
  • a turn in the back line, where they learn to defend.

The kids who are quick to the ball will always find it. Rotations are for the ones who don't yet.

Hot days, injuries and late arrivals

  • Hot days: shorten the stints. Changing every 4 minutes instead of 6 gives more rests, and the minutes stay even.
  • Injuries: take the player off and share their remaining stints among the rest. Injured players are outside the minimum in the national rules, but log what they played anyway.
  • Late arrivals: add them at the next change. They share the time that's left from when they arrive.

Telling parents

Before the first game, tell parents your league's minimum and what you aim for. For example: "Everyone plays at least three quarters' worth, and we rotate zones every quarter." Then share the minutes after the game. If someone sees their child on the interchange at the start of a quarter, the tally shows they're still getting their share. See talking to parents about playing time for wording you can use.
