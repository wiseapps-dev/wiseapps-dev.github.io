A Soccer 7v7 and 9v9 Substitution Plan

In 7v7 soccer (two 20-minute halves) with a change every 5 minutes, a squad of 10 shares eight stints: six players get 30 minutes and four get 25. In 9v9 (two 25-minute halves), a squad of 12 plays 37:30 each. Keepers take a half each, and that time counts as playing time.

Updated 2026-11-21. Written by the maker of Benchwise.

Small-sided soccer is made for equal game time. Changes can happen at any time, there's no limit on how many you make, and the games are short enough to plan on one page. This guide covers the formats used in Australian junior football, how many minutes each player gets for every squad size, how to handle keepers, and a sample rotation you can copy.

The formats

Football Australia's national MiniRoos playing formats set the small-sided game for under 6 to under 11. Football Victoria's 2026 MiniRoos guidelines use the same numbers.

Age group Format On the field + subs Keeper Game length
Under 6 and 7 4v4 4 + up to 3 No 2 × 20 minutes
Under 8 and 9 7v7 7 + up to 4 Yes 2 × 20 minutes
Under 10 and 11 9v9 9 + up to 5 Yes 2 × 25 minutes

Half-time is 5 minutes, and there's no offside in any of these formats. In Victoria, no results are recorded for MiniRoos, and clubs are asked not to make a big deal of scores.

States, associations and summer competitions sometimes vary the length or numbers, so check your local rules.

The substitution rules

The national formats say players can be rotated on and off at any time, even while the ball is in play, as long as the player coming off is off the field before the substitute goes on. Football Victoria adds that changes should happen often, so all players get about the same amount of playing and resting time.

On equal time, Football Victoria strongly encourages clubs to give every player a go on match day. Ideally that means equal game time each week, or across the season, but it's left to the club and coach. Football Australia's game time policy template, which clubs can adopt, goes further for under 6 to under 11:

  • equal game time, with players rotated across both halves so nobody plays only one half
  • every player rotated through every position, including goalkeeper, for example in goal for one half and outfield for the other.

From under 12, the template suggests a minimum of half a game, with extra time earned.

7v7: minutes for every squad size

Two 20-minute halves with 7 on the field gives 280 player-minutes. Change about every 5 minutes and each half has 4 stints, so the game has 8 stints and 56 places to share.

Squad Fair split (5-minute stints)
8 everyone plays 35
9 2 play 35, 7 play 30
10 6 play 30, 4 play 25
11 1 plays 30, 10 play 25
12 8 play 25, 4 play 20

The keeper's time in goal counts as playing time. MiniRoos 7v7 allows up to 4 subs, so 11 is the most you'd have under those rules. A squad of 12 suits other competitions.

9v9: minutes for every squad size

Two 25-minute halves with 9 on gives 450 player-minutes. Changing every 5 minutes gives 10 stints. Changing a little less often, every 6 minutes 15 seconds, splits each half into 4 even stints, which is easier to remember: 0:00, 6:15, 12:30 and 18:45. That gives 8 stints and 72 places.

Squad Fair split (6:15 stints)
10 2 play 50:00, 8 play 43:45
11 6 play 43:45, 5 play 37:30
12 everyone plays 37:30
13 7 play 37:30, 6 play 31:15
14 2 play 37:30, 12 play 31:15

MiniRoos 9v9 allows up to 5 subs, so 14 is the most you'd have under those rules.

Keepers

Keeping goal is a different job, so treat it differently:

  • A half each. Put a keeper in for a whole half. Swapping keepers mid-half means gloves, bibs and a nervous kid jogging to goal while play is live.
  • It's still playing time. Count the keeper's half as minutes on the field. The planner then gives them less outfield time in the other half, so it stays fair.
  • Everyone gets a turn. Following the Football Australia template, rotate goalkeeping through the whole squad over the season. A child who loves it can have more turns, but make sure others get a go too.
  • Not two halves in a row. Don't put the same child in goal for both halves unless they ask.

Six players get 30 minutes and four get 25. Next week, give the extra stint to four or more different players. Everyone plays in both halves, and there are only six change times in the game. You can open this exact chart, add your own names and print it from the 7v7 rotation for 10 players page.

Making rolling changes work on the day

  • Change at a stoppage anyway. Even though you can change with the ball in play, a throw-in or goal kick is calmer and safer for the kids.
  • Warn the next players. About a minute before a change, tell the kids coming on who they're replacing and where to play.
  • Off first, then on. It's the rule, and it stops you having eight on the field.
  • Give a parent the clipboard. A helper reading out the next change frees you to watch the game.
  • Mix up positions. When a sub comes on, they don't have to take the exact spot of the player coming off. Use the change to move kids between defence, midfield and attack.

When the plan breaks

A late arrival joins at the next change and shares the time that's left. An injured player comes off, and their remaining stints are shared among the rest. If a change runs late, give the player who missed out a little extra later on. Keep a season tally, so a rough week is balanced the next week.
