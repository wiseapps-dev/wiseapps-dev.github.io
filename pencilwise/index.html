Handwriting practice in school letter styles

Pencilwise is a handwriting app for children aged about 4 to 8. Your child watches each letter being written, traces it with a finger from a green start dot, then writes it alone. It checks every stroke, in styles taught in the US, the UK, every Australian state, New Zealand, Germany, France and Spain, or a common school print.

The same letter in six of the app’s styles: print, pre-cursive and cursive. Pick one to watch it written.

Watch, trace, then write

  1. Watch, then trace

    An animated pencil writes the letter stroke by stroke. Then your child traces it, starting at the green dot and following the arrows. A stroke that goes the wrong way gets a gentle hint and a replay.

  2. Write it alone, earn stars

    The guide fades and your child writes the letter on the lines. Pencilwise checks the order, direction and shape of each stroke, with a forgiving tolerance for small hands, and gives up to three stars.

  3. Their own name

    Type a first name and trace it letter by letter, then see it written all together in your child’s own strokes. Make printable worksheets of it too.

15 letter styles

Schools teach different letter shapes: a k with a loop or without, a y that curls or slants, exit flicks, cursive loops. Pencilwise draws every letter from stroke data, in each model’s stroke order and direction. The US, UK and NSW & ACT styles, the German Grundschrift and French school cursive are drawn to follow those models’ published formation rules. The other Australian states (Victoria with WA and the NT, Queensland, South Australia, Tasmania), New Zealand and Spanish school cursive are drawn from openly licensed fonts made in the style of those models. School print is a common print with the letters of your child’s language; it isn’t any country’s official model.

Not included: Italian, Polish, Portuguese and Dutch school cursive and the German joined scripts. No style is endorsed by a school authority; pick the one closest to what your child’s teacher uses.
