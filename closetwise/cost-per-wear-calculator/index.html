Cost per wear calculator

Cost per wear is the price of a piece divided by the number of times you wear it. Enter what you paid, how often you wear it each month and how long you expect to keep it, and the calculator shows what each wear costs and how many wears reach a dollar each.

Any currency works. Wears are whole days: wearing a piece twice in one day counts once, as in the app.

A worked example

A pair of sneakers costs $120. You wear them 8 days a month and expect them to last two years. That is 8 × 24 = 192 wears, and $120 ÷ 192 = $0.63 a wear. A $60 dress worn to two weddings costs $30 a wear, almost fifty times as much.

The blazer cost more than the sneakers but has been worn a tenth as often, so each wear costs more than ten times as much. That is the point of the number: it shows which pieces earn their place.

How to use the result

  • Before you buy, estimate honest wears. If a piece only works for one occasion, put in the number of those occasions a year.
  • For pieces you own, count real wears. Guesses run high; a log doesn’t.
  • To compare, look at the wears needed for a target, such as $1 a wear, rather than the price alone.
