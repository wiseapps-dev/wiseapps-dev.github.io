Sneaker rotation and rest days

Rotating sneakers means not wearing the same pair two days running, so each pair can dry out between wears. Feet have around 250,000 sweat glands, and the Royal College of Podiatry advises rotating shoes for exactly this reason. Two or three pairs and a rest day or two suit most people.

Updated 21 November 2026

Why shoes need a rest

Feet sweat more than you might think. The Royal College of Podiatry notes that there are more sweat glands per inch in our feet than anywhere else in the body, about 250,000 of them, and its advice for sweaty feet includes not wearing the same footwear every day and rotating shoes so they have a chance to dry out.

A damp pair smells sooner, and leather and suede that stay damp can stiffen or lose their shape. A pair that gets a day off has time to dry through.

How many rest days

There is no single right number, but these are sensible starting points:

  • Canvas and mesh (most runners and casual sneakers): one rest day.
  • Leather and suede: two rest days, more after rain.
  • Pairs worn for sport or long walks: at least one full day, with the insoles out.

In Closetwise, rest days are a setting. A pair worn on Monday with two rest days rests on Tuesday and Wednesday and is ready again on Thursday.

How many pairs you need

Two pairs that you alternate is the minimum rotation. Three gives each pair two days off without any planning. Collectors with many more pairs have the opposite problem: some pairs are never worn at all, which you can see by sorting by wears.

Help them dry

  • Loosen the laces and pull the tongue forward when you take them off.
  • Take out removable insoles after a long or wet day.
  • Use cedar shoe trees or plain newspaper for leather pairs.
  • Let them dry at room temperature, away from heaters and direct sun.

Track the rotation, not just the pairs

A rotation only works if you can tell at a glance what you wore and when. A simple list on your phone works, or a shoe rack that sorts pairs by how long they've rested. Tracking wears also gives you cost per wear for each pair, which is the honest answer to "do I really need another pair?"
