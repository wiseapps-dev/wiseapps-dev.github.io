How to build a capsule wardrobe

A capsule wardrobe is a small set of clothes that all work together, often around 30 pieces for a season. Build it from what you already wear most: count your real wears, choose two or three base colours, keep pieces that mix with each other, and fill genuine gaps slowly.

Updated 21 November 2026

The idea is older than it sounds. The term appeared in American publications as early as the 1940s; Susie Faux, who ran a London boutique called Wardrobe, revived it in the 1970s; and Donna Karan's 1985 collection "7 Easy Pieces" made it famous by showing how a handful of pieces could be combined into many outfits.

The point has stayed the same: fewer pieces, all of which go together, so getting dressed is quick and nothing sits unworn.

Start with what you already wear

Don't start by shopping. Start by finding out what you actually reach for. WRAP's Valuing our clothes research found that around 30% of the clothing in the average UK wardrobe had not been worn for at least a year. Your capsule is probably already in your wardrobe, mixed in with that 30%.

For two to four weeks, note what you wear each day. The pieces that come up again and again are the core of your capsule. The ones that never come up are candidates for the not-worn-in-90-days list.

Count your wears without trying

Closetwise logs a wear in one tap, or every piece of an outfit at once. After a few weeks, the most-worn list is the core of your capsule.

Choose two or three base colours

Look at the core pieces and find the colours they share: often navy, black, grey, camel, denim or white. Make the bottoms, outerwear and shoes from those. Tops, scarves and the odd dress can carry colour and prints, because they only need to go with the base, not with each other.

Set a number, but treat it as a tool

Courtney Carver's Project 333 challenge is to wear just 33 items for three months. A fixed number forces choices and makes gaps obvious. If 33 is too few for your life, pick a number that is, then hold to it for a season and see what you missed.

Check that every piece works three ways

For each piece, name three others it goes with. A top that only works with one skirt is a costume, not a capsule piece. Lay outfits out, or build them on a canvas, and the odd ones out show themselves quickly.

Fill gaps slowly

When something is genuinely missing, say a coat that works with everything, buy one piece, not five. Before you buy, estimate how often you'll wear it and work out the cost per wear. A piece that will be worn twice a week for years can cost more up front and still be the cheapest thing you own.

Review at the end of each season

Look at what you wore, what you didn't, and what you wished you had. Swap seasonal pieces in and out, keep the base, and repeat. After a year, the capsule fits your real life rather than a picture of it.
