How to declutter with a “not worn in 90 days” list

List every piece you haven't worn in the last 90 days, then decide on each one: wear it this week, keep it for a known season or occasion, or let it go. The list works because it is built from what you actually wore, not from what you remember wearing.

Updated 21 November 2026

Why 90 days

Ninety days is about a season: long enough that anything you like has had a chance to be worn, short enough that the list stays current. The Minimalists put it as two questions: have you used it in the last 90 days, and if not, will you use it in the next 90? If not, it's okay to let it go.

Most wardrobes have plenty to find. WRAP's research found that around 30% of the clothing in the average UK wardrobe had not been worn for at least a year.

Build the list

You need to know when each piece was last worn. Three ways to get there:

  • The hanger trick. Turn every hanger backwards. When you wear something, hang it back the right way. After 90 days, the backwards hangers are your list.
  • A note on your phone. Write down what you wore each day. It takes ten seconds and catches folded clothes and shoes, which the hanger trick misses.
  • A closet app. Log a wear when you get dressed, and let it keep the list.

Decide piece by piece

Go through the list with each piece in your hands, and give it one of three answers:

  1. Wear it this week. If you hesitated, plan an outfit around it now. If it still doesn't get worn, you have your answer.
  2. Keep it for a known reason. A winter coat in summer, a swimsuit before a beach trip, a suit for weddings. Name the season or the occasion.
  3. Let it go. Sell it, give it away, pass it to a friend, or find a textile recycling point near you.

Repairs count as a fourth answer only if you book them now. A pile of things to fix one day is just a second wardrobe.

Keep it honest

The list is only as good as your wear log. Log wears for a few weeks before your first clean-out, and include shoes, bags and accessories: they hide on the list more than anything else.

Then look at what survived. Those pieces are the start of a capsule wardrobe, and their cost per wear will fall every time you reach for them.
