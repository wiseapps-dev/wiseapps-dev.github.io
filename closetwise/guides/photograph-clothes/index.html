How to photograph clothes for a closet app

Lay or hang each piece against a plain background that contrasts with it, in soft daylight, with the whole piece in frame and nothing overlapping it. Background removal can then separate it cleanly. Shoot shoes from the side as a pair, and tidy any leftover edge with a quick touch-up.

Updated 21 November 2026

A closet app is only as pleasant as its pictures. Clean cut-outs on one background make a closet you can scan at a glance, like a catalogue. Busy photos of clothes on a bed make it look like laundry. The difference is mostly in how you take the photo.

Pick a plain background with contrast

Background removal looks for the edge between the piece and what's behind it. Give it an easy edge: a plain door, a wall, a floor or a sheet in a colour that contrasts with the piece. Dark clothes on a light background, light clothes on a dark one.

The hardest case is white on white. In Closetwise's own tests of its background removal, a white tee on an off-white background was the weakest result by far, while pieces on plain, contrasting backgrounds were separated almost perfectly. If you have a lot of white and cream, keep a dark sheet for them.

Use soft daylight

Shoot near a window during the day, without the flash. Soft light shows the colour of the fabric truly and avoids hard shadows, which can be mistaken for part of the piece. Avoid a patch of direct sun across half the garment.

Get the whole piece in, and only the piece

Frame the whole garment with a little space around it. Smooth it flat, fold the sleeves in neatly or let them hang, and move anything that overlaps it: other clothes, a bag strap, your feet.

Hangers are the most common leftover. Lay pieces flat where you can, or take them off the hanger and hang them from a hook out of frame.

Shoes: from the side, as a pair

Shoot shoes from the side, at shoe height, with both shoes together and the toes pointing the same way. That is how a shelf shows them, and it is the view you'll recognise at a glance in a shoe rack.

Do it in batches

Photograph a category at a time: all the tops, then all the trousers. You'll find a setup that works and repeat it. In Closetwise you can pick several photos from your library and add them together, then review each cut-out.

Fix what's left

No cut-out is perfect every time. If part of the background or a hanger survives, a touch-up with erase, restore and crop fixes it in seconds. Closetwise keeps the original photo, so you can always go back and cut it out again.
