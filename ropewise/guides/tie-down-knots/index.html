Tensioning knots: tarps, guy lines and bundles

To tension a tarp ridgeline or clothesline, anchor one end with a round turn and two half hitches, then pull the other end tight with a trucker's hitch. It works like a simple pulley, so you can pull the line far tighter than by hand, then lock it off. For guy lines you'll re-tension, use a taut-line hitch.

Trucker's hitch, to pull a line tight

Partway along the line, make a loop to act as a pulley. Take the end round the tree, post or rail, back up through the loop, and pull down: the loop gives you real pulling power. Pinch the line at the loop, then finish with two half hitches below it. To release it, untie the half hitches.

Two things to watch: the rope wears where it runs through the loop, and a slipped loop like this one can jam or slip under heavy loads.

How to tie a trucker's hitch

Anchoring the other end

Tie the first end to the tree, post or ring with a round turn and two half hitches. It holds a steady pull and you can untie it even under load. A bowline round a post works too, and unties easily afterwards.

Taut-line hitch, for lines you'll adjust

An adjustable loop for tent and tarp guy lines: slide the hitch along the standing part to tighten or slacken the line, and it holds where you leave it. It can slip in slippery synthetic cord; add an extra wrap inside the loop if it does.

How to tie a taut-line hitch

Bundles and bags

  • Constrictor knot: grips very hard once tightened. Good for a bundle of poles or the neck of a sack, but it can jam so tight it has to be cut off.
  • Reef knot: ties the two ends of a cord round a parcel. It lies flat and unties easily, but it's not for loads.

Vehicle and trailer loads

Take care. Not a substitute for rated tie-downs. For loads on a vehicle, roof rack or trailer, use rated tie-down straps and follow your local load-restraint rules.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.
