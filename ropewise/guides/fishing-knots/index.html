Fishing knots: which knot for hooks, leaders and reels

Updated 22 November 2026

The fishing knots worth learning first are a hook knot, a line-to-line join and a reel knot. For hooks, tie a Palomar in braid, or an improved clinch or uni knot in mono. To join braid to a leader, use a double uni, or an FG knot for the slimmest join. Spool a reel with an arbor knot.

Hook, lure and swivel knots

Knot Line Why pick it
Palomar knot Braid, mono, fluoro Simple, and well suited to braid. Awkward with big lures.
Uni knot Mono, fluoro, braid One method for hooks, joins and snells. Add turns in braid.
Improved clinch knot Mono, fluoro The classic, quick with practice. Not for braid.
San Diego jam knot Mono, fluoro Compact; seats well in heavier fluoro.
Snell knot Mono, fluoro, braid Bait and circle hooks: a straight pull down the shank.
Non-slip loop knot Mono, fluoro A small loop so a lure keeps its action.

Joining line to line

Knot Joins Why pick it
Double uni knot Most lines, braid to mono Easy to tie on the water. Bulkier through the guides.
FG knot Braid to a mono or fluoro leader Very slim and smooth. Slow to tie.
Albright knot Light line to much heavier line Copes with very different thicknesses.
Blood knot Two monos of similar size Slim and neat. Fiddly.
Surgeon's knot Leader to tippet Very quick and simple.

Reel and rig knots

  • Arbor knot: ties new line to an empty spool. With braid on a smooth spool, put a layer of mono or tape under it first.
  • Dropper loop: a loop in the middle of the line that stands out to the side, for a second hook or a sinker.

Tying any fishing knot well

  • Wet the knot before you pull it tight, then snug it down slowly and evenly.
  • Use extra turns in braid: it's slippery, and knots like the uni need them to grip.
  • Trim the tag only once the knot is seated.
  • Test every knot with a hard pull before you cast. Cut and retie any that slips.
