How to tie two ropes together

Updated 22 November 2026

To tie two ropes together, use a sheet bend: it's quick, works even when the ropes are different sizes, and unties easily afterwards. Leave long tails, because it can shake loose when the ropes go slack. Don't join ropes that take a load with a reef knot: it can capsize and slip apart.

The sheet bend, for most jobs

Make a bight in the end of the thicker rope, bring the thinner rope up through it, round behind both legs, and tuck it under itself. It's quick to tie, copes with ropes of different thickness or stiffness, and is easy to untie after loading. Use it to lengthen a line in a hurry, or to bend a heaving line to a mooring line.

Its weakness is that it can shake loose when it isn't under load. Leave long tails, and check that both short ends finish on the same side. For slippery or very unequal ropes, take the end round the bight a second time before you tuck it: that's a double sheet bend.

Why not a reef knot?

The reef knot (square knot) looks like a join, but it's a binding knot: it ties the two ends of one cord round a parcel or a sail, where it lies flat and unties easily. Used to join two ropes under load, especially ropes of different sizes, it can capsize and slip apart. Keep it for tying parcels and bundles.

Joining fishing line

Fishing line needs slimmer joins that pass through the rod guides:

  • Double uni knot: easy to tie, even on the water, and works with most lines.
  • FG knot: a very slim join from braid to a mono or fluoro leader.
  • Albright knot: joins a light line to a much heavier one.
  • Blood knot: two monos of similar thickness.
  • Surgeon's knot: a quick join from leader to tippet.
