Best knot for tying a rope to a post, rail or ring

For most jobs, the best knot for tying a rope to a post, rail or ring is a round turn and two half hitches. The round turn takes the strain, the half hitches lock it, and you can untie it even under load. For a quick tie, use a clove hitch; to drop a loop over a post, a bowline.

Round turn and two half hitches, the all-rounder

Take the end round the ring or rail twice, then tie two half hitches round the standing part, both the same way. It holds a steady pull, and because the round turn takes the strain, you can untie it while it's loaded: handy for a mooring line or a fender line.

It's bulkier and slower to tie than a single hitch, and the half hitches can loosen if the line is left slack and shaken.

Take care. Tie both half hitches the same way and pull them snug. A slack line that is shaken about can loosen them, so check mooring lines regularly.

How to tie a round turn and two half hitches

Clove hitch, quick and adjustable

Two turns round a rail, with the end tucked under the diagonal. It's very quick to tie and untie, and easy to adjust before you load it, which is why it's used to hang fenders from a guardrail.

It can slip or roll off when the pull changes direction or the post turns. For anything important, add a half hitch round the standing part.

Take care. It can work loose when the pull changes direction or the post turns. For a mooring line, or anything that matters, finish with half hitches or use a round turn and two half hitches.

How to tie a clove hitch

Bowline, a loop over a post or bollard

Tie a fixed loop in the end of the rope, then drop it over a pile or bollard, or tie it round a ring or a post. It's easy to untie after loading, but it can shake loose when it isn't loaded.

Take care. A bowline can shake loose when it isn't loaded. Leave a long tail or back it up with a stopper knot, and check it each time you use it.

How to tie a bowline

For particular posts and rings

  • A horn cleat on a dock or deck: the cleat hitch is quick to make and quick to cast off, even under load.
  • An anchor ring or mooring buoy: the anchor bend is less likely than a round turn and two half hitches to work loose when the line is jerked and slackened over and over.
  • A tree, for a tarp ridgeline: the Siberian hitch is fast to tie and releases with one pull on the end.
  • A log or bundle to drag along the ground: the timber hitch tightens as you pull, and loosens as soon as the pull eases.
  • A pull along a rail or spar: the rolling hitch grips when the pull runs along the object.
  • A tent peg: the taut-line hitch slides to tension a guy line.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.
