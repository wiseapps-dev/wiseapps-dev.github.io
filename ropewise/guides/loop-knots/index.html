Loop knots: how to tie a fixed loop in a rope

Updated 22 November 2026

For a fixed loop at the end of a rope, tie a bowline: it unties easily even after loading, but it can shake loose when slack, so leave a long tail. Fishing line has its own loops: the dropper loop, which stands out from the middle of the line, and the non-slip loop for lures.

Bowline, the loop that unties

A fixed loop that unties easily, even after a heavy load. Drop it over a bollard, tie it round a ring or a post, or put a loop in the end of a sheet. It can shake loose when it isn't under load.

Take care. A bowline can shake loose when it isn't loaded. Leave a long tail or back it up with a stopper knot, and check it each time you use it.

How to tie a bowline

Dropper loop, a loop in the middle of fishing line

Tied without an end, it makes a loop that stands out to the side of the line, for a second hook or a sinker. Keep the twists even on both sides, and pull both ends so the twists close up round the loop.

How to tie a dropper loop

Non-slip loop, a loop for a lure

A small fixed loop through a lure's eye, so the lure can swing and wobble freely instead of being held stiff by the knot. It takes more steps than a clinch, and judging the loop size takes practice.

How to tie a non-slip loop

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.
