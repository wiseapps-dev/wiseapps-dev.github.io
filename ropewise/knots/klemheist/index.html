How to tie a Klemheist hitch

Klemheist hitch. A friction hitch tied with a cord loop round a thicker rope. It grips when the loop is pulled one way and slides when unloaded. It is used in climbing as a one-way grip on a rope. The cord must be thinner than the rope, and the grip must be tested.

Life-safety knot. How well it grips depends on the cord's diameter and material and on the rope. Use cord sold for friction hitches, thinner than the rope, and follow the maker's guidance. Test it carefully before relying on it, and learn friction hitches from a qualified instructor.

Origin and references

Origin
A friction hitch derived from the Prusik, also called the French Machard. It matches a knot Ashley showed for fastening a loop to a pole (#1762), though he did not name it or describe its grip; the sources checked name no inventor.

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Loading it the wrong way. It grips in one direction only: load only the hanging end, downward.
  • Using cord as thick as the rope. The cord must be thinner; add wraps if it slips.

See also

  • Figure-eight loop: A fixed loop tied in a doubled rope, easy to check because it shows two parallel figure eights.
  • Alpine butterfly: A fixed loop tied in the middle of a rope. It takes load on the loop or along the rope in either direction.
  • Double fisherman's: Joins two ropes, or the ends of one cord into a loop, with a double overhand from each end round the other.
