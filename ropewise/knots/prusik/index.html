How to tie a Prusik hitch

Prusik hitch. A friction hitch: a loop of cord wrapped round a rope that grips when loaded and can be slid by hand when the load is off. It is used in climbing and mountaineering, and to hold a tarp on a ridgeline. Test the grip every time: the cord and the rope both matter.

Learn them from a qualified instructor, and have your knots checked, before you rely on one. Follow your equipment maker's instructions.

Life-safety knot. How well it grips depends on the cord's diameter and material and on the rope. Use cord sold for friction hitches, thinner than the rope, and follow the maker's guidance. Test it before relying on it, and learn friction hitches from a qualified instructor.

Origin and references

Origin
Named after Austrian mountaineer Karl Prusik, who published it in a 1931 Austrian manual on climbing a rope. Ashley later showed the same structure, unnamed, as a hitch on a spar (#1763).

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Using cord as thick as the rope. The cord must be thinner, or it will not grip.
  • Using it untested. Load the legs and test the grip every time.
