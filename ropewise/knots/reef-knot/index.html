How to tie a reef knot

Reef knot. A flat binding knot that ties the two ends of a cord snugly around a bundle or parcel. Use it for tying the ends of a cord around a parcel, bag or bundle, or reefing or tying in a sail.

  • Easy
  • 5 steps
  • Home & garden knots

Take care. This knot can slip or capsize. Don't use it to lift, join ropes under load or secure anything heavy.

Step by step

The two colours are the two ropes or lines. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

At a glance

Use
Ties a parcel or bundle.
  • Tying the ends of a cord around a parcel, bag or bundle
  • Reefing or tying in a sail
  • Tying the ends of a scarf, apron strings or a cloth wrap
Good
Lies flat against the bundle, is easy to check and easy to untie.
Watch out
Not for joining two ropes: it can capsize and slip apart under load, especially with ropes of different size.
Unties
Yes, easily
Tie it in
Rope or cord
Difficulty
Easy, 5 steps
Also called
Square knot

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Joining two ropes that will take a load. A reef knot can capsize and slip apart.
  • Tying a granny knot. Cross right over left, then left over right, so both ends lie on the same side.

Origin and references

Origin
At least 4,000 years old; the ancient Greeks knew it as the Hercules knot. The name comes from reefing sails and was in print by 1794.
