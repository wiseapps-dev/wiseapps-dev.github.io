How to tie a surgeon's knot

Surgeon's knot. A quick join made by tying a double overhand knot in two overlapping lines held together. Use it for adding tippet to a fly leader, or joining a leader to a main line of a different thickness. Very quick and simple, and copes with lines of different thickness.

At a glance

Use
Quick leader-to-tippet join.
  • Adding tippet to a fly leader
  • Joining a leader to a main line of a different thickness
Good
Very quick and simple, and copes with lines of different thickness.
Watch out
A little bulky, and the whole tippet or line must pass through the loop each time.
Unties
No: cut it off and retie
Tie it in
Mono or fluoro
Difficulty
Easy, 5 steps
Also called
Double surgeon's knot

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in. Wet it, snug it down slowly and test it with a hard pull before you fish it.

Common mistakes

  • Making only one pass. Take both lines through the loop twice.
  • Pulling the ends unevenly. Wet it and pull all four ends evenly.

Origin and references

Origin
Named after surgeons' use of the double first throw to keep tension on a suture; anglers tie the same double overhand in two overlapping lines as a quick join.
