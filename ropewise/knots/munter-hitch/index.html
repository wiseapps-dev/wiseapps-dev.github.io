How to tie a Munter hitch

Munter hitch. A friction hitch on a carabiner that lets rope run under control in either direction. Climbers use it as a belay hitch on a pear-shaped (HMS) locking carabiner. It twists the rope, and how to handle the rope must be learnt from a qualified instructor.

  • Medium
  • 6 steps
  • Climbing knots

Climbing knots. These knots are used in climbing and other activities where a mistake can kill. Ropewise shows how each knot is tied. It doesn't teach climbing and doesn't replace instruction. Learn them from a qualified instructor, and have your knots checked, before you rely on one. Follow your equipment maker's instructions.

Life-safety knot. Use it only on a suitable pear-shaped (HMS) locking carabiner, with the gate locked and the brake strand kept off it: rope running over the gate can unscrew or open it. Learn how to handle the rope from a qualified instructor before you rely on it.

Not a substitute for instruction. Not for life-safety use without training.

Origin and references

Origin
Italian climbers Mario Bisaccia, Franco Garda and Pietro Gilardoni developed it in the late 1950s as the mezzo barcaiolo, and the UIAA recognised it in the late 1960s after the Italian Alpine Club promoted it. Swiss guide Werner Munter popularised it in the 1970s, hence the name; it is also called the Italian hitch or HMS.

Common mistakes

  • Using a small or D-shaped carabiner. It needs a wide, pear-shaped (HMS) locking carabiner.
  • Learning the rope handling from a diagram. How to hold and feed the rope must be taught by a qualified instructor.
