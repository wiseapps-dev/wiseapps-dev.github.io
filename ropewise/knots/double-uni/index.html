How to tie a double uni knot

Double uni knot. Two uni knots, each tied round the other line, slid together to join two fishing lines. Use it for joining a mono or fluoro leader to your main line, or joining braid to mono. Easy to tie, even on the water, and works with most line types and sizes.

Step by step

The two colours are the two ropes or lines. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in. Wet it, snug it down slowly and test it with a hard pull before you fish it.

Origin and references

Origin
Two uni knots tied round each other's line. The uni knot was published by Ashley as a noose (#1121), developed as a fishing knot by Norman Duncan in the early 1960s and named the uni knot by Vic Dunaway in 1970.

Common mistakes

  • Too few turns in braid. Braid needs extra turns to grip.
  • Pulling the main lines before each knot is closed. Pull each end to close its own turns first, then slide the knots together.
