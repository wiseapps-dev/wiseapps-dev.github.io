How to tie a Siberian hitch

Siberian hitch. A quick-release hitch for tying a line to a tree or post, released in one pull. Use it for tying one end of a tarp ridgeline to a tree, or tying a tarp or clothesline to a post so it releases with one pull.

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

Also called
Evenk hitch

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Origin and references

Origin
Also called the Evenk hitch. Wikipedia says it was recorded among the Nenets people of northern Russia in the early 1990s; Animated Knots describes it as a slipped figure eight tied round the standing part.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the end long where it can snag. One tug on the end releases the hitch.
  • Leaving it loose. Pull the standing part to snug it against the tree.

See also
