How to tie a double fisherman's knot

Double fisherman's. Joins two ropes, or the ends of one cord into a loop, with a double overhand from each end round the other. Climbers use it to make a loop of accessory cord, for example for a Prusik hitch. It is compact, but very hard to untie after loading.

Life-safety knot. Leave long tails and check that each side is a full double overhand before use. For climbing, learn it from a qualified instructor and have it checked.

Origin and references

Origin
An ancient bend: Heraklas described it in the first century AD, and multi-turn forms turn up in old Inuit baleen lines. Ashley lists the double form as #294; it is also called the grapevine bend.

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Tying the two halves differently. They should be the same knot turned end for end: four parallel lines on one side, two Xs on the other.
  • Cutting the tails short. It uses a fair length of each end.
