How to tie a trucker's hitch

Trucker's hitch. A tensioning hitch that works like a simple pulley, so you can pull a line far tighter than by hand, then lock it off. Use it for tensioning a tarp ridgeline or clothesline between trees, or pulling a tarp or cover tight over a woodpile.

  • Fiddly
  • 7 steps
  • Camping & outdoor knots

Take care. Not a substitute for rated tie-downs. For loads on a vehicle, roof rack or trailer, use rated tie-down straps and follow your local load-restraint rules.

At a glance

Use
Tensions a line or tarp.
  • Tensioning a tarp ridgeline or clothesline between trees
  • Pulling a tarp or cover tight over a woodpile
  • Cinching gear or firewood bundles tight
Good
Gives real pulling power for very tight lines, and unties easily once the half hitches are off.
Watch out
The rope wears where it runs through the loop; a slip-knot loop can jam or slip under heavy loads.
Unties
Yes, once the half hitches are off
Tie it in
Rope or cord
Difficulty
Fiddly, 7 steps
Also called
Waggoner's hitch

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Losing the tension while tying off. Pinch the line at the loop before the half hitches.
  • Relying on the slipped loop under a heavy load. It can jam or slip.

Origin and references

Origin
Geoffrey Budworth traces it to carters and hawkers with horse-drawn vehicles; Ashley lists it as #2124. It has many names, including waggoner's, harvester's and lorry hitch.
