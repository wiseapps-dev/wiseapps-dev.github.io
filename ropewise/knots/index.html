List of knots and their uses

These are the 40 knots in Ropewise, grouped into chapters by use, from fishing and boating to camping, home and garden, and laces and craft. Each entry says what the knot is for. Open any knot for its diagrams, how it unties and the mistakes to avoid.

Fishing knots 13

  • Albright knotJoins a light line to a much heavier one by wrapping the light line round a loop doubled in the heavy line.
  • Arbor knotTies new line to an empty reel spool: an overhand round the standing line, locked by a stopper knot in the tag.
  • Blood knotA slim, neat join for two mono lines of similar thickness: turns on each side, with both ends tucked through the middle.
  • Double uni knotTwo uni knots, each tied round the other line, slid together to join two fishing lines.
  • Dropper loopA loop tied in the middle of a line that stands out to the side, for adding an extra hook or sinker.
  • FG knotA very slim braid-to-leader join: the braid is woven round the leader in alternating wraps, then locked with half hitches.
  • Improved clinch knotThe classic way to tie mono to a hook, lure or swivel: turns round the standing line, locked by a double tuck.
  • Non-slip loop knotA small fixed loop through a lure's eye, so the lure can swing and wobble freely instead of being held stiff by the knot.
  • Palomar knotA doubled line through the eye, an overhand knot, then the loop passed over the hook. Simple to tie.
  • San Diego jam knotTurns taken back over a doubled line, then the tag tucked through the loops at both ends. A compact hook knot.
  • Snell knotTurns of line bound round the hook shank, so the pull comes straight down the shank. A favourite for bait and circle hooks.
  • Surgeon's knotA quick join made by tying a double overhand knot in two overlapping lines held together.
  • Uni knotTurns taken round a doubled line inside a loop: one easy method that ties line to hooks, lures and swivels.

Boating knots 8

  • Anchor bendA round turn through a ring with the end tucked back under both turns, finished with a half hitch. Made for anchor rings.
  • BowlineA fixed loop at the end of a rope that is easy to untie, even after loading.
  • Cleat hitchMakes a line fast to a horn cleat with a turn round the base, a figure-eight over the horns and a locking hitch.
  • Clove hitchTwo turns round a rail or post with the end tucked under the diagonal. Quick to tie and easy to adjust.
  • Figure-eight stopper knotA bulky stopper in the end of a rope that stops it running out through a block, fairlead or cleat.
  • Rolling hitchA hitch that grips a rail, pole or spar against a pull along its length, toward the side with the round turn.
  • Round turn and two half hitchesA round turn through a ring or round a rail takes the strain, and two half hitches round the standing part lock it.
  • Sheet bendJoins two ropes, even of different thickness or stiffness: the thinner rope wraps round a bight in the thicker one.

Camping & outdoor knots 4

  • Siberian hitchA quick-release hitch for tying a line to a tree or post, released in one pull.
  • Taut-line hitchAn adjustable loop for guy lines: slide the hitch along the standing part to tighten or slacken the line, and it holds where you leave it.
  • Timber hitchA quick hitch for dragging a log or bundle along the ground: a loop that tightens as you pull and grips with a few twists of the end.
  • Trucker's hitchA tensioning hitch that works like a simple pulley, so you can pull a line far tighter than by hand, then lock it off.

Home & garden knots 5

  • Constrictor knotA hard-gripping binding knot: two turns with the end trapped under a riding turn, so it clamps down hard.
  • Double overhand knotAn overhand knot with one extra turn, giving a fatter stopper that is less likely to pull through a hole.
  • Overhand knotThe simplest knot: a small lump near the end of a rope or cord that stops it slipping through a hole.
  • Reef knotA flat binding knot that ties the two ends of a cord snugly around a bundle or parcel.
  • Slip knotAn overhand knot tied with a bight of the working end, so one pull on the end releases it.

Climbing knots 7

Climbing knots. These knots are used in climbing and other activities where a mistake can kill. Ropewise shows how each knot is tied. It doesn't teach climbing and doesn't replace instruction. Learn them from a qualified instructor, and have your knots checked, before you rely on one. Follow your equipment maker's instructions.

Not a substitute for instruction. Not for life-safety use without training.

  • Alpine butterflyA fixed loop tied in the middle of a rope. It takes load on the loop or along the rope in either direction.
  • Double fisherman'sJoins two ropes, or the ends of one cord into a loop, with a double overhand from each end round the other.
  • Figure-eight follow-throughA figure eight tied in the rope, threaded through the harness and retraced to form the tie-in loop.
  • Figure-eight loopA fixed loop tied in a doubled rope, easy to check because it shows two parallel figure eights.
  • Klemheist hitchA friction hitch tied with a cord loop round a thicker rope. It grips when the loop is pulled one way and slides when unloaded.
  • Munter hitchA friction hitch on a carabiner that lets rope run under control in either direction.
  • Prusik hitchA friction hitch: a loop of cord wrapped round a rope that grips when loaded and can be slid by hand when the load is off.

Laces & craft knots 3

  • Elastic bracelet knotThe standard knot for closing a stretchy bead bracelet: a reef knot with an extra turn in the first throw so it holds while you finish it.
  • Ian knotA very fast way to tie the standard shoelace bow: make two loops at once, then pull each lace through the other loop.
  • Sliding bracelet knotAn adjustable closure for cord bracelets: one end is coiled round the other cord so the knot slides to open and close.
