How to tie a non-slip loop knot

Non-slip loop knot. A small fixed loop through a lure's eye, so the lure can swing and wobble freely instead of being held stiff by the knot. Use it for tying on lures, jigs and flies for more natural action, or tying a leader straight to a lure instead of using a snap.

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

Loop that lets a lure swing.
  • Tying on lures, jigs and flies for more natural action
  • Tying a leader straight to a lure instead of using a snap
Good
The loop doesn't close up under load, so the lure keeps its action.
Watch out

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in. Wet it, snug it down slowly and test it with a hard pull before you fish it.

Origin and references

Origin
Developed by angler and writer Lefty Kreh as a variant of the Homer Rhodes loop, and published in Practical Fishing Knots, which he co-wrote with Mark Sosin; it is often called the Kreh loop.
References
  • Non-Slip Mono Loop (Animated Knots by Grog)

Common mistakes

  • Passing the tag back through the overhand on the wrong side. It goes back in on the side it came out (steps 3 and 5).
  • Too few turns in light line. Use more turns the lighter the line.
