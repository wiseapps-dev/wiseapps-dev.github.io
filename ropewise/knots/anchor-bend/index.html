How to tie an anchor bend

Anchor bend. A round turn through a ring with the end tucked back under both turns, finished with a half hitch. Made for anchor rings. Use it for tying an anchor line (rode) to the anchor ring or shackle, or making a line fast to a mooring buoy ring.

Take care. Leave a long tail and check the knot every time you anchor. On a line that stays in place, seize or tape the end to the standing part. If it comes undone, the boat can drift.

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

At a glance

Use
Rope to an anchor ring.
  • Tying an anchor line (rode) to the anchor ring or shackle
  • Making a line fast to a mooring buoy ring
  • Bending a line to a ring that will be wet and jerked about
Good
Less likely than a round turn and two half hitches to work loose when the line is jerked and slackened over and over.

Origin and references

Origin
A traditional seaman's knot for bending a line to an anchor ring; 'bend' here keeps its old meaning of 'tie to'. It is also called the fisherman's bend; Animated Knots cites Ashley #1841.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving out the tuck under both turns (step 3). That tuck, close to the ring, is what makes it an anchor bend.
  • Leaving a short tail. Leave a long one, and seize the end to the standing part on a permanent anchor line.
