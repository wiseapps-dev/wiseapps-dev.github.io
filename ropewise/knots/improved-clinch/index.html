How to tie an improved clinch knot

Improved clinch knot. The classic way to tie mono to a hook, lure or swivel: turns round the standing line, locked by a double tuck. Use it for tying a hook, lure or swivel to monofilament, or tying on a fly with a light tippet.

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

Origin and references

Origin
A traditional angling knot of unknown origin, also called the fisherman's or salmon knot; it is the clinch knot with the end tucked through the big loop as well.

Common mistakes

  • Missing the second tuck. After the small loop by the eye, the end goes back through the big loop too (step 4).
  • Using it in braid, which can slip. Use a Palomar knot for braid.

See also

  1. Palomar knot: A doubled line through the eye, an overhand knot, then the loop passed over the hook. Simple to tie.
  2. Uni knot: Turns taken round a doubled line inside a loop: one easy method that ties line to hooks, lures and swivels.
  3. San Diego jam knot: Turns taken back over a doubled line, then the tag tucked through the loops at both ends. A compact hook knot.
