How to tie a constrictor knot

Constrictor knot. A hard-gripping binding knot: two turns with the end trapped under a riding turn, so it clamps down hard. Use it for closing the neck of a sack or bag, or holding a bundle of sticks, poles or hoses together.

At a glance

Use
Binds a sack or bundle tight.
  • Closing the neck of a sack or bag
  • Holding a bundle of sticks, poles or hoses together
  • Whipping a rope end temporarily before cutting it
Good
Grips very hard once tightened; quick to tie with practice.
Watch out
Can jam so tight it has to be cut off; not much grip on flat or square objects.
Unties
Often not: it may have to be cut
Tie it in
Cord or rope
Difficulty
Medium, 4 steps

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Tying it where you will want to untie it. It can grip so hard that it has to be cut off.
  • Using it on a flat or square object, where it has little grip.

Origin and references

Origin
Clifford Ashley named it in The Ashley Book of Knots (1944) and implied he had devised it decades earlier, but it appears in earlier sources, including a French carpentry treatise from 1841.
