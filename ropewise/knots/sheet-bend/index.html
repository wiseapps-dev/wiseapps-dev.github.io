How to tie a sheet bend

Sheet bend. Joins two ropes, even of different thickness or stiffness: the thinner rope wraps round a bight in the thicker one. Use it for joining two lines of different sizes, such as a heaving line to a mooring line, or lengthening a line in a hurry.

  • Medium
  • 5 steps
  • Boating knots

Take care. It can shake loose when the ropes go slack. Leave long tails, check both short ends are on the same side, and use a double sheet bend for slippery or very unequal ropes.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

  • Tying a line to a rope eye or loop (becket)
Good
Quick to tie, works with unequal ropes, and is easy to untie after loading.
Watch out

Difficulty

Also called
Becket bend, Weaver's knot

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Finishing with the short ends on opposite sides. Tuck the end so both short ends are on the same side.
  • Making the bight in the thinner rope. The bight goes in the thicker rope, and the thinner one wraps round it.

Origin and references

Origin
An ancient bend, also called the weaver's knot, used in netting since Neolithic times and still used by weavers to mend warp threads. Steel's seamanship manual mentions it in 1794.
