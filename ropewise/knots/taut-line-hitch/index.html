How to tie a taut-line hitch

Taut-line hitch. An adjustable loop for guy lines: slide the hitch along the standing part to tighten or slacken the line, and it holds where you leave it. Use it for tensioning tent and tarp guy lines around a peg, or tightening a clothesline or ridgeline.

Also called
Tautline hitch

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Putting the last turn inside the loop. The final turn goes outside it, towards the standing part.
  • Using slippery synthetic cord without an extra wrap. Add one inside the loop if it slips.

Origin and references

Origin
An early use of the name is Riley's 1912 Knots, Hitches, and Splices; the Boy Scout Handbook adopted it in 1948. The form drawn here is a rolling hitch tied round the standing part (Ashley #1856); Ashley's midshipman's hitch (#1855) is the tucked variant.
