How to tie a dropper loop

Dropper loop. A loop tied in the middle of a line that stands out to the side, for adding an extra hook or sinker. Use it for adding a second hook to a bait or paternoster rig, or hanging a sinker or fly off the main line.

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

At a glance

Use
Side loop for a second hook.
  • Adding a second hook to a bait or paternoster rig
  • Hanging a sinker or fly off the main line
Good
Tied without needing an end, and the loop stands out from the line, which helps stop tangles.
Watch out
Takes practice to keep the twists even, and is fiddly to tie in heavy line.
Unties
No: cut it off and retie
Tie it in
Mono or fluoro
Difficulty
Medium, 3 steps
Also called
Blood loop, Blood loop dropper knot

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in. Wet it, snug it down slowly and test it with a hard pull before you fish it.

Origin and references

Origin
A traditional angler's mid-line loop, also called the blood loop or blood dropper loop. Neither source checked documents its origin.

Common mistakes

  • Uneven twists. Keep the twists even on both sides of the loop.
  • Pulling the loop first. Pull both ends so the twists close up round it, then pull the loop.
