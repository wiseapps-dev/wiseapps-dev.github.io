How to tie a clove hitch

Clove hitch. Two turns round a rail or post with the end tucked under the diagonal. Quick to tie and easy to adjust. Use it for hanging fenders from a guardrail or stanchion, or making a line fast to a post or rail for a short time.

  • Easy
  • 5 steps
  • Boating knots

Take care. It can work loose when the pull changes direction or the post turns. For a mooring line, or anything that matters, finish with half hitches or use a round turn and two half hitches.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

Also called
Builder's hitch

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Using it alone when the pull changes direction or the post can turn. It can slip or roll off: add a half hitch.
  • Tucking the end on the wrong side. The two ends should leave in opposite directions (step 4).

Origin and references

Origin
The name first appears in Falconer's marine dictionary of 1769, but the knot is far older: art from the early 16th century shows it in ships' ratlines.
