How to tie a Palomar knot

Palomar knot. A doubled line through the eye, an overhand knot, then the loop passed over the hook. Simple to tie. Use it for tying braid straight to a hook, lure or swivel. Easy to tie, even in poor light, and well suited to braid.

  • Easy
  • 4 steps
  • Fishing knots

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

At a glance

Use
Braid to a hook or lure.
  • Tying braid straight to a hook, lure or swivel
  • Drop-shot rigs: leave a long tag to hold the sinker
Good
Easy to tie, even in poor light, and well suited to braid.
Watch out
Uses more line than most hook knots, and the loop must pass over the hook or lure, which is awkward with big lures and trebles.
Unties
No: cut it off and retie
Tie it in
Braid, mono or fluoro
Difficulty
Easy, 4 steps

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in. Wet it, snug it down slowly and test it with a hard pull before you fish it.

Common mistakes

  • Leaving the loop on the eye. Pass it right over the hook so it sits round the shank behind the eye (step 3).
  • Doubling too little line. Double about 15 cm so the loop clears the hook or lure.

Origin and references

Origin
Developed by Chester 'Chet' Palomar of Pomona, California, and introduced at the Fred Hall fishing show in 1971.
