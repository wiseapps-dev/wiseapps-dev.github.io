How to tie a bowline knot

Bowline. A fixed loop at the end of a rope that is easy to untie, even after loading. Use it for dropping a loop over a bollard or pile to moor a boat, or tying a line round a ring, post or tree.

  • Medium
  • 5 steps
  • Boating knots

Take care. A bowline can shake loose when it isn't loaded. Leave a long tail or back it up with a stopper knot, and check it each time you use it.

Also called
King of knots

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Forming the small loop the other way round. Lay the working side over the top, as in step 1, or the knot will not form properly.
  • Leaving it where the line flaps slack. A bowline can shake loose when it is not loaded: leave a long tail or back it up.

Origin and references

Origin
An old sailor's knot named after the bowline, a rope that held the edge of a square sail forward. Its earliest known printed mention is John Smith's A Sea Grammar (1627).
