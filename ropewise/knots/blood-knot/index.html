How to tie a blood knot

Blood knot. A slim, neat join for two mono lines of similar thickness: turns on each side, with both ends tucked through the middle. Use it for joining two lengths of mono of similar thickness, or building tapered fly leaders and adding tippet.

Step by step

The two colours are the two ropes or lines. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in. Wet it, snug it down slowly and test it with a hard pull before you fish it.

Origin and references

Origin
Its origin is unknown. Keith Rollo is credited with calling the barrel knot a 'blood knot', and Stanley Barnes named the wrapping method in his 1951 book on anglers' knots.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing the two ends through different gaps. Both go through the same middle gap, from opposite sides.
  • Using it for braid or for lines of very different thickness. Use a double uni or an Albright knot instead.
