How to tie a rolling hitch

Rolling hitch. A hitch that grips a rail, pole or spar against a pull along its length, toward the side with the round turn. Use it for tying a line to a rail, pole or spar when the pull runs along it.

  • Medium
  • 5 steps
  • Boating knots

Take care. It grips only when the pull runs along the object towards the round turn. Test it with a gentle pull first, and don't rely on it on smooth or slippery surfaces.

At a glance

Use
Grips against a lengthwise pull.
  • Tying a line to a rail, pole or spar when the pull runs along it
Good
Holds against a lengthwise pull along the object, and slides to adjust when unloaded.
Watch out
Only grips when the pull is along the object toward the round turn; may slip with slippery synthetic rope or on smooth rails. To hitch to another rope, Ashley's tucked version (#1735) grips better; it isn't drawn here.
Unties
Yes, once the load is off
Tie it in
Rope or cord
Difficulty
Medium, 5 steps

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Making the round turn on the wrong side. Both turns go towards the pull (steps 1 and 2).
  • Pulling across the rail. It only grips when the pull runs along it, towards the round turn.

Origin and references

Origin
Around 1800 this knot was called the Magnus or Magner's hitch; Richard Henry Dana's The Seaman's Friend (1841) gave it the modern name. The version drawn here is Ashley's #1734; his #1735 adds a tucked turn for rope-to-rope use.

See also

  • Anchor bend: A round turn through a ring with the end tucked back under both turns, finished with a half hitch. Made for anchor rings.
