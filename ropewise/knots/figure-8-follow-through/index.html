How to tie a figure-eight follow-through

Climbing knots. These knots are used in climbing and other activities where a mistake can kill. Ropewise shows how each knot is tied. It doesn't teach climbing and doesn't replace instruction. Learn them from a qualified instructor, and have your knots checked, before you rely on one. Follow your equipment maker's instructions.

Life-safety knot. Retrace it fully and dress it neatly. Leave a long tail, at least 10 cm (about a hand-width); follow your instructor and harness maker. Some instructors teach a backup knot: follow your instructor. Have a partner check it every time, before every climb, and learn it from a qualified instructor.

Not a substitute for instruction. Not for life-safety use without training.

Figure-eight follow-through. A figure eight tied in the rope, threaded through the harness and retraced to form the tie-in loop. It is the usual knot climbers use to tie into a harness, and it is easy to check by eye, because two parallel strands follow the figure-eight shape.

Origin and references

Origin
A traditional knot with no known inventor: the finished knot is the figure-eight loop (Ashley #1047), tied by threading the end round the harness and retracing a figure eight. It is the standard tie-in taught by climbing bodies today.

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Not retracing it all the way. The end must follow the first figure eight exactly, over and under, until it comes out beside the standing part.
  • Skipping the check. Dress it with no crossed strands and have a partner check it before every climb.
