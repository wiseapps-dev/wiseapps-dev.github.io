How to tie a slip knot

Slip knot. An overhand knot tied with a bight of the working end, so one pull on the end releases it. Use it for a temporary stopper you can release in a hurry, or holding a coil or bundle together until you need to free it.

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

At a glance

Use
Quick-release stopper.
  • A temporary stopper you can release in a hurry
  • Holding a coil or bundle together until you need to free it
  • A quick-release finish on a cord or drawstring
Good
Usually releases with a single tug on the end.
Watch out
Can come undone if the end gets caught or tugged. Put the load on the standing part only.
Unties
Yes: one tug on the end
Tie it in
Rope, cord or laces
Difficulty
Easy, 3 steps
Also called
Slipped overhand knot

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Origin and references

Origin
A traditional slipped overhand knot; Ashley (#529) held that it is the only knot properly called a slip knot.
Ashley Book of Knots
#529

Common mistakes

  • Putting the load on the end. Load the standing part only.
  • Leaving the end where it can catch. One pull on it releases the knot.
