How to tie a uni knot

Uni knot. Turns taken round a doubled line inside a loop: one easy method that ties line to hooks, lures and swivels. Use it for tying a hook, lure or swivel to mono, fluoro or braid. Easy to learn, works in most lines, and the same method also joins lines (double uni) and makes snells.

Step by step

Blue is the standing part and yellow is the working end. A dashed red arrow shows where the end goes next.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

At a glance

Use
Hook, lure or swivel, any line.
  • Tying a hook, lure or swivel to mono, fluoro or braid
  • Leave it unsnugged as a loop so a lure can swing freely
Good
Easy to learn, works in most lines, and the same method also joins lines (double uni) and makes snells.
Watch out
Needs extra turns in slippery braid, and must be wetted and snugged down neatly or it can slip.
Unties
No: cut it off and retie
Tie it in
Mono, fluoro or braid
Difficulty
Easy, 4 steps
Also called
Grinner knot, Duncan loop

Every knot weakens the line it is tied in. Wet it, snug it down slowly and test it with a hard pull before you fish it.

Origin and references

Origin
Ashley published it in 1944 as a noose (#1121); Norman Duncan developed it independently as a fishing knot in the early 1960s (the Duncan loop), and Vic Dunaway popularised it as the uni knot in 1970.

Common mistakes

  • Too few turns in braid. Add extra turns.
  • Snugging it dry or unevenly. Wet it, close the turns with the tag, then slide the knot down to the eye.
