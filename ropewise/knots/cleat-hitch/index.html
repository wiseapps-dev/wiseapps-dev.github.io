How to tie a cleat hitch

Cleat hitch. Makes a line fast to a horn cleat with a turn round the base, a figure-eight over the horns and a locking hitch. Use it for tying a mooring line to a dock or deck cleat, or making fast a sheet or other line on a cleat.

  • Easy
  • 6 steps
  • Boating knots

Take care. Use a cleat that suits the rope, and finish with one locking hitch: extra turns can jam it. Check mooring lines again as the wind and tide change.

Practise first and check the knot every time. Not for climbing or for holding a person.

Every knot weakens the rope it is tied in, and any knot can slip or jam. Check it each time you tie it.

Common mistakes

  • Adding extra figure-eights or hitches. They add nothing and can jam the hitch: one locking hitch is enough.
  • Using a cleat that is too small for the rope.

Origin and references

Origin
A traditional nautical hitch for making a line fast to a horn cleat (Ashley #1615); no inventor is documented in the sources checked.
